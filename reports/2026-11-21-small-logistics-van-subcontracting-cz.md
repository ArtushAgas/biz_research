# Small logistics company: IKEA-type delivery and carrier subcontracting (owner-requested analysis)

Date 2026-11-21. Built from three independent research reports (market/competition, costs/unit economics, legal). FX about 24.3 CZK/EUR [estimate]. Research summary, not legal advice. Not a formal judge score; the indicative level is in the 40s, comparable to other thin-margin service ideas screened so far (cargo-bike courier 27, warehouse-worker supply 32).

## Bottom line
- A thin margin is acceptable, but this business is both capital-heavy and labour-heavy. "Runs without the owner driving" means paid drivers, so profit is about 10-20% of revenue before overhead and swings hard with the day rate.
- No carrier publishes subcontractor rates, so the single most important number (revenue per van-day) is unverified. The only public anchor is Rohlik's recruiting claim of up to 4,100-4,500 CZK/day invoiced by OSVČ couriers.
- At 4,800 CZK/day, 3 vans and about 10 owner hours a week give about 1,275 EUR/month pre-tax. At 4,500 CZK/day it needs 4-5 vans; a 5-10% rate cut wipes out the profit.
- IKEA is not directly reachable: one big contractor serves each region (HRAL), PPL does small parcels, and assembly goes to named firms.
- Recommended path: a low-capital test as overflow subcontractor (one leased van or a few self-employed drivers) for an existing carrier, using the owner's logistics contacts, and get written rate quotes before buying vehicles.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low | 1 | 4,500 | about -6k CZK | 9.7% |

At 90% van utilisation the base case barely reaches 1,000 EUR. Owner time: 10-13 h/week for 3 vans. Overhead assumed 15k CZK/month.

Cash: three used vans about 1.65M CZK plus about 900k CZK receivables and buffer = about 2.55M CZK (about 105k EUR, just over the cap); with leasing about 1.0M CZK (about 42k EUR). Payback about 40-100 months. Payment terms of 30-90 days are typical [estimate].

## 4. Three models
1. **Own vans + employed drivers (subcontract to a carrier):** the table above. Capital-heavy; concentration risk (one or two carriers).
2. **Asset-light (self-employed drivers with own vans, you resell routes):** pay about 3,800-4,200, resell at 4,800-5,500 = 700-1,300 CZK/day commission (12-20%) [estimate]; little capital. Big risk: false self-employment (Rohlik couriers case, NSS 22 Ads 168/2025-51; a ban on business up to 2 years since 2025; Labour Code change planned). Carriers such as Rohlik already recruit OSVČ directly, so the intermediary layer is exactly what they cut.
3. **Heavy-item delivery + assembly (two-person crew):** cost about 5,975 CZK/van-day; at 800-1,200 CZK per delivery and 7-9 drops a day revenue is 5,600-10,800 CZK, margin 0-45% [estimate]. Margin comes from B2C marketing and assembly, not from subcontracting, so it is less "self-running". Gas or electrical connection of appliances is a regulated craft.

## 5. Legal (research summary)
- **Licence:** carrying goods for hire in a van up to 3.5 t, even domestically, is a koncesovaná živnost "Silniční motorová doprava – nákladní vnitrostátní ≤3.5 t" (Act 111/1994 §4; Trade Act Annex 3 as amended by Act 217/2022). On the agent's reading, professional competence and the EUR 9,000 financial standing apply only above 3.5 t (and international 2.5-3.5 t); responsible-representative requirement unverified. Fee and timing unverified.
- **Parcel delivery:** possibly a postal service under Act 29/2000; ČTÚ notification and fines up to 10M CZK for operating without it; contested, so a lawyer or ČTÚ opinion is needed first. Working as a subcontractor under a licensed carrier shifts but does not remove the question.
- **Employees:** Labour Code applies to domestic van driving; tachographs apply to international 2.5-3.5 t from 1.7.2026.
- **Subcontract liability:** no statutory joint liability for subcontractor wages in transport (Labour Code §324a covers construction); recipient VAT guarantee (§109 ZDPH) makes counterparty and bank-account checks important. Agency-work licence if you lend drivers.
- **Entity:** the VAT-payer s.r.o. (B2B customers, input VAT on vans). Burden: Medium for domestic ≤3.5 t; High for parcel delivery and anything above 3.5 t.
- **Ops:** no Prague low-emission zone; free parking for low-emission plates ended 1.1.2026; vignette for motorways.

## 6. Risks
Unknown day rate; customer concentration and 5-10% rate cuts; payment terms; driver shortage and wage pressure; fuel volatility; švarcsystém and postal-service classification; liability for damaged goods; Q4 peak vs quiet months; low switching cost for buyers.

## 7. What would make it work
Written commitment of 3+ van-days a day at 4,800+ CZK each, payment within 30 days, drivers at or below 40,000 CZK gross, and a first contract from the owner's own network.

## 8. 30-day test (about 500-1,500 EUR, no fleet purchase)
1. Ask 5-8 carriers and subcontract dispatchers in your network (PPL/DPD sub-contractors, regional depots, HRAL, the IKEA assembly firms, Alza or Rohlik carrier desks) for written per-day or per-stop rates, volumes, payment terms and penalties.
2. Ask the živnostenský úřad and a lawyer about the ≤3.5 t concession, the parcel/postal question and the OSVČ structure; request insurance quotes (CMR/cargo, havarijní).
3. Run a one-van overflow pilot for the Q4 peak (rented or leased van, a hired or OSVČ driver) to measure real utilisation, delay and payment time.
4. Decision rule: proceed only with a written rate of at least 4,800 CZK/van-day (or equivalent), 30-day payment, and a second customer.

## 9. Data gaps
