Legal and regulatory

Research summary, not legal advice. EUR-Lex and e-Sbírka were unreachable, so findings rest on snippets. Verify with a lawyer or accountant.

  • Li-ion batteries: importer is probably a producer (ECOBAT, ISOH2 reporting, ~300-1,000 EUR/yr [estimate]). Exclude.
  • Descaler: CLP labelling, Czech SDS, notification, possible biocide status. Exclude.
  • Brew groups and gaskets: food-contact declarations (Reg. 1935/2004) may apply.
  • GPSR: EU responsible person required (~150-600 EUR/yr [estimate]).
  • Trademarks: "compatible with" wording only; Chinese "original" parts are likely counterfeit.
  • Reminders: opt-in under Act 480/2004 s.7, fines to CZK 10m; GDPR applies.
  • Right to repair: Czech bill had a second reading on 2026-06-24; final adoption unconfirmed. Weak support for this idea.
  • Start-up legal and compliance: 2,000-6,000 EUR [estimate]. Burden medium; medium-to-low for non-electrical robot parts.

Top risks

  1. Price compression with no moat: the same factories supply the Kaufland sellers.
  2. Customer acquisition cost is unmeasured.
  3. No Czech demand sizing; Spareka's French results do not transfer.
  4. Brand takedowns plus battery, descaler and brew-group compliance.
  5. Dead stock from 10+ new models a year against 50-500 piece MOQs.

30-day test (under 1,000 EUR)

  1. Week 1: Czech search volume and CPC for the top 30 models; Heureka and Kaufland best-seller ranks.
  2. Week 1: samples and written quotes from 3-5 suppliers; ask distributors about originals.
  3. Week 2: landing page or Shoptet trial with 10 SKUs, one finder page, two videos; accountant check of the compliance list.
  4. Weeks 2-4: 300-500 EUR Google Ads; measure CPA, add-to-cart and reminder opt-ins.
  5. Week 4 go/no-go: contribution before ads of 8+ EUR, CPA of 4 EUR or less, credible path to 170 orders/month.

Stop if Spareka announces Czech entry.
